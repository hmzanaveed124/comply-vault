## Strategic Messaging Rules

Highest-priority instructions for any marketing skill using this context:

1. **Lead with the problem, not AI.**

2. **Lead with supervision, not archiving.**

3. **Position ComplyVault above the existing stack, not as a replacement for it.**

4. **Describe the outcome as defensible supervisory evidence, not generic automation.**

5. **Humans remain accountable; AI routes attention and assists review.**

6. **Do not manufacture urgency through regulatory fear.**

Something happens → communication gets archived → later someone asks what happened → humans reconstruct it from systems, notes, emails, and memory.

**ComplyVault alternative:** Supervision with a preserved decision trail.

### Problem → Existing solution → Missing layer

**Problem:** RIAs retain huge volumes of communications and meeting evidence, but supervisory review and documentation remain human-heavy.

**Existing stack:** Archive + email + CRM + meeting tools + compliance procedures.

**Missing layer:** A system that helps identify what deserves attention, records supervisory decisions, connects those decisions to source evidence, and preserves the resulting chain.

**ComplyVault:** That missing supervisory evidence layer.

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** ComplyVault is a supervisory evidence layer for RIAs — it sits above the firm's existing archive so compliance can find what needs attention and prove how it was handled.

**What it does:** Sits above the systems that already retain communications and meeting evidence. Helps identify what deserves review, connects source evidence to supervisory decisions, runs human review gates (advisor → compliance manager → CCO where applicable), and preserves a sealed evidence pack showing what required attention, what decision was made, and how it was resolved. Capture paths include Zoom auto-ingest and file upload; email evidence can be linked where ingested. Does **not** replace the archive or become another system of record.

**Product category:** Lead with **supervisory evidence layer for RIAs**. Plain-English expansion: RIA compliance software that sits above the existing archive.

**Product type:** B2B SaaS

**Business model:** Subscription — Essentials **$299/mo**, Professional **$799/mo** (commercial anchor), Enterprise custom. Implementation available from $2,500 (waived with 6-month or annual commitment). UK list prices exist (£249 / £649) but UK/FCA is a side market — do not lead GTM with them. Do **not** use older $149/$249 pricing. Primary conversion: book a demo / sample audit pack.

## Target Audience

### Direct ICP

**Primary commercial wedge:** Independent RIAs with lean internal compliance capacity where the CCO, principal, or outsourced compliance provider is still manually reviewing retained communications and assembling supervisory evidence.

**Broader strategic ICP:** Compliance-constrained RIAs that already retain the required communications, but still rely on people to determine what deserves review, document supervisory decisions, and reconstruct evidence later.

**Initial sweet spot:** Typically independent RIAs with \~1–20 advisers and lean compliance capacity — **initial target range, not a hard qualification rule.** Qualify primarily on supervisory workload and evidence fragmentation rather than adviser count or AUM. A 30-adviser firm with one overwhelmed CCO can still be a fit. Do **not** frame ICP as simply “small RIAs that need compliance software.”

**Trigger characteristics (prefer these over firmographics):**

- They already have an archive

- Compliance reviews samples manually

- Evidence lives across multiple systems

- The CCO (or equivalent) has to reconstruct “what happened and what we did about it”

- Examinations or audits create retrieval work

- Adding clients/advisers increases compliance workload almost linearly

- They don’t want another archive migration

**Geography:** US investment advisers — SEC-registered and state-registered RIAs. UK/FCA is a side market for now; keep product architecture expansion-ready but don’t dilute US positioning with FCA terminology.

**Decision-makers (direct):** CCO / Head of Compliance; Principal / Founder (often wearing the CCO hat); COO / Operations — do not assume a single persona.

**Primary use case:** Find activity that deserves compliance attention, document the supervisory decision, and preserve evidence of how it was handled — without migrating off the existing archive.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Surface what actually needs review amid retained communications

- Connect source evidence to supervisory decisions and preserve the chain

- Stop reconstructing “what happened and what we did about it” after the fact

- Keep the archive; add the layer that makes supervision defensible

**Use cases:**

- Ongoing supervisory review where sampling is still manual

- Exam / audit retrieval of supervisory decisions tied to source evidence

- Meeting and correspondence evidence sealed into exportable packs

- Multi-firm supervisory evidence for outsourced CCO / consultant client books

### Sales qualification (operational)

Use to distinguish a “good RIA” from a **good ComplyVault prospect**. Qualify when most of the following are true:

- Has an existing archive or retention system

- Has recurring supervisory review workload

- Review / evidence assembly is meaningfully manual

- There is a named compliance owner (CCO, principal wearing the hat, or outsourced provider)

- Buyer does not want archive replacement

- Some supervisory decision trail must be demonstrated internally or to examiners

### Disqualify / deprioritize when

Operational disqualification (distinct from strategic anti-persona):

- No meaningful review volume

- No retained digital communications

- Buyer wants only an archive

- Broker-dealer surveillance is the primary need

- No compliance owner

- Buying requirement is autonomous compliance decisioning

### Channel / Distribution ICP

Strategically distinct from direct ICP — economics differ (one relationship → many RIAs). Influences content, outbound, partnerships, and product architecture.

- Outsourced CCO firms

- Compliance consultancies

- RIA platforms

- TAMPs

- Technology / integration partners serving multiple RIAs (e.g. AdvizorStack-class relationships)

A direct sale wins one firm. A channel relationship **can** expose ComplyVault to many firms — figures like 10, 50, or 100+ are **illustrative of potential reach, not expected outcomes or forecasts**.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Firms already retain the required communications, but people still determine what deserves review, document supervisory decisions, and reconstruct evidence later — capacity doesn’t scale with growth.

**Why alternatives fall short:**

- Archives prove a record existed; they don’t prove what required attention or how it was resolved

- Manual review + spreadsheets + inbox + CRM + Word/PDF + human memory doesn’t scale

- Advisor productivity / meeting tools optimize notes, not supervisory evidence

- Rip-and-replace compliance platforms force archive migration firms don’t want

**What it costs them:** Linear growth in compliance workload as clients/advisers are added; exam retrieval fire drills; incomplete story of supervisory decisions; pressure to hire before the process is fixed

**Emotional tension:** Knowing the archive is “complete” but still unable to confidently answer what was reviewed, decided, and resolved

### Competitive frame (three arenas)

**1. Dominant competitor — status quo / manual supervision (and reconstruction):** Archive + spreadsheets + inbox + CRM + manual review + Word/PDF evidence + human memory. This is the most common real sales competitor.

**2. Named vendors to monitor:** Hadrius, Archive Intel, Greenboard. Overlap with ComplyVault varies by workflow and product evolution; compare against specific use cases rather than assuming full category equivalence. Avoid fighting on their category terms.

**3. Adjacent products currently include:** Zocks, Jump, and adviser productivity / meeting-intelligence tools — useful for notes and retention today; adjacency can change as products evolve. They are not (today) a supervisory evidence layer above the archive.

**Territory to own:**

**Switching proposition (critical):**

> Don’t switch. Add ComplyVault above what you already have.

> Keep the systems that retain your records. Add the layer that helps you supervise them.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- Sits **above** the existing archive — no archive migration

- Supervisory evidence: what deserved attention → decision → resolution, tied to source evidence

- Replaces supervision-by-reconstruction with a preserved decision trail

- Sealed evidence packs with immutable custody posture (Object Lock / RecordSeal / supersession without erasure) — **SHIPPED** where that surface applies

- Three-layer human review where the firm’s process requires it — AI assists, humans decide

- Completes the stack beside tools firms already use; intentionally not another system of record

- Firm-specific compliance controls (e.g. Compliance Cockpit disclosure profile) where shipped

**How we do it differently:** Route compliance attention; connect evidence to decisions; preserve how issues were handled — instead of summarizing meetings or re-archiving communications.

**Why that's better:** Examinations and day-to-day supervision need the decision trail, not just retained messages.

**Why we expect buyers to choose us / buying thesis (HYPOTHESIS — not yet win/loss validated):** They refuse another archive migration and need capacity to supervise what they already retain.
